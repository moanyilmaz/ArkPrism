\title{\mytooltitle}

\begin{abstract}
Static taint analysis for \openharmony/\arkts depends on identifying privacy-sensitive sources before propagation begins. A strict namespace--method source loader does not preserve enough evidence for property reads, package migration between \texttt{@ohos.*} and \texttt{@kit.*}, or calls whose sensitive identity is exposed through a manager/helper receiver. A missed identity suppresses the initial taint fact regardless of the downstream solver.

We present \mytool, a static analysis framework that combines evidence-carrying API identity resolution, ArkTS-specific IR recovery, bounded asynchronous path recovery, and provenance-annotated audit subgraphs. Identity resolution records namespace-constrained package migration, receiver origin or type, access kind, and IR-lowered aliases. The IFDS analysis recovers initializers, lexical captures, exceptional flow, and access paths; a separate post-IFDS phase handles selected callback and Promise/\texttt{async-await} gaps and retains their provenance rather than presenting them as native IFDS edges. Lifecycle entries are ordered and explicitly bounded to avoid manufacturing flows through unrestricted event cycles.

A source-first audit samples 60 real projects by scale and configured-import presence, reviews every candidate occurrence before consulting detector output, and establishes 90 gold occurrences in 16 projects plus 44 confirmed zero projects. \mytool recovers all 90 at their exact source locations with no additional reports (100\% observed precision and recall; 95\% Wilson lower bound 95.91\%). On a 64-case identity benchmark, the complete resolver is the only evaluated configuration to classify all positive and matched-negative patterns. Across all 67 cases of the author-released HapBench suite, \mytool attains 100.00\% precision, 94.34\% recall, and 97.09\% F1; its strongest comparative advantage over HapFlow is specificity (100.00\% versus 71.43\%). A 48-case asynchronous benchmark further isolates nine Promise-\texttt{then} recoveries ($p=0.00390625$). Finally, a single-build study of 1,014 projects characterizes detector evidence, typed may-paths, robustness, and analysis cost.
\end{abstract}
